Tide pools are the quietest argument for patience I know. Twice a day the sea
walks out and leaves a dozen small worlds behind, each one complete: a hermit
crab dragging its borrowed shell over the corrugated rock, a green anemone
closing like a fist around whatever drifts too close, and the stubborn winkles
that glue themselves to the stone and wait for the water to come back.

The waiting is the whole trick. Nothing in a tide pool hurries, because nothing
in a tide pool can hurry and survive. A barnacle that opens early dries out by
noon; a limpet that wanders when the sun is up becomes lunch for a gull. So they
time themselves to a clock older than any of us, and they get on with the small,
slow business of staying alive.
